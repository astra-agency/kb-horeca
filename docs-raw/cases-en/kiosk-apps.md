# Case Study: Self-Service Kiosk Applications

## Context

At large resort hotels, the front desk often becomes a bottleneck for trivial requests: guests queue to check their account balance, ask what was charged at lunch, or transfer resort credit to a spouse across the property. These are purely informational requests — they consume staff time that should be directed at service, not data lookup.

## Objective

Deploy touchscreen kiosks throughout the property so guests can self-serve routine requests at any time, without queuing or involving staff.

## What Was Done

### 1. Guest Identification via NFC Wristband

The guest approaches a kiosk and taps their NFC wristband to the reader. The kiosk loads their profile instantly — no card, no PIN, no paper.

### 2. Kiosk Functionality

#### Resort Credit & Internal Currency Management

Many resort and all-inclusive properties operate an internal spend system: guests load credit at check-in and spend at restaurants, bars, spas, and activity desks without carrying wallets or cards.

The kiosk provides:
- **Balance check** — current credit remaining on the account,
- **Transaction history** — itemised list of where and when charges were made,
- **Credit transfer** — send credit to another guest profile (e.g. give a child spending money for the gift shop, or split a balance with a partner).

#### Information & Property Directory

Additional kiosk functions:
- today's activities and entertainment schedule,
- property map with directions to facilities,
- restaurant menus and opening hours,
- current promotions and special offers.

### 3. Kiosk Placement Strategy

Kiosks are placed at high-traffic points where informational requests naturally arise: restaurant entrance, pool deck, lobby, activity hub, and beach bar. The guest resolves the question where the question occurs — not at the front desk.

### 4. System Integration

Each kiosk connects in real time to:
- **PMS** — guest booking and profile data,
- **Resort credit system** — live balance and transaction feed,
- **Activities management system** — real-time event schedule.

## Results

- Front desk call volume for routine informational requests drops measurably after kiosk deployment.
- Guests value the transparency: seeing every transaction in real time builds trust and eliminates billing disputes.
- The credit transfer function proves unexpectedly popular with families — eliminating arguments about who has money at which point on the property.

## Key Considerations

- **Kiosks require ongoing maintenance:** screen cleaning, NFC reader calibration, and uptime monitoring are non-negotiable operational responsibilities.
- **UI simplicity is paramount.** The target user ranges from an 8-year-old accessing their activity credit to an elderly guest checking their meal plan balance. The interface must be self-explanatory with zero training.
- **Accessibility compliance:** in the US, ADA requirements apply to self-service kiosks (height, tactile elements, audio output for visually impaired users). In the EU, the European Accessibility Act (EAA) comes into full effect in 2025.
- **A fallback path must always exist:** if a kiosk is offline or the wristband is unreadable, the guest must have an immediate alternative (mobile app, front desk, secondary kiosk).
- **Security:** the credit transfer function requires a confirmation step (e.g. tap wristband again or enter a PIN) to prevent unauthorised transfers.

## Hardware Considerations

| Component | Specification notes |
|---|---|
| Display | 21–27" commercial-grade touchscreen; IP54 rated for outdoor/poolside units |
| NFC reader | ISO 14443 / ISO 15693 compatible; integrated flush with housing |
| Connectivity | Wired ethernet preferred; Wi-Fi as backup |
| Housing | Vandal-resistant; powder-coated steel or marine-grade aluminium for outdoor |
